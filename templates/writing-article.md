Opening paragraph. Begin with the idea, tension, or observation that gives the reader a reason to continue. Do not repeat the article title as a heading.

A second paragraph can establish context, clarify the argument, or explain why the subject matters in practical terms.

## First Section Heading

Use second-level headings for the main sections of the article. Keep paragraphs focused and reasonably short.

The page title is generated from front matter, so article content should never contain an `# H1` heading.

### Optional Subsection

Use third-level headings only when they make a longer section easier to navigate.

## Lists

Use a bulleted list when sequence does not matter:

- A clear observation or recommendation.
- A second item with enough context to stand on its own.
- A final item that completes the thought.

Use a numbered list for an actual sequence:

1. Define the problem.
2. Test the assumptions.
3. Build the smallest useful system.
4. Measure what changes.

## Emphasis and Quotations

Use **bold text** sparingly for emphasis and *italics* for a subtle change in voice.

> Blockquotes should highlight a meaningful principle or argument, not decorate an ordinary sentence.

Add links with descriptive language, such as [the source documentation](https://example.com), rather than generic text like “click here.”

## Closing Section

End with a useful conclusion, implication, or question. Avoid generic summaries that merely repeat every section above.
